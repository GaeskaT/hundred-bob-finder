Product design · a bettor-facing companion to The Hundred Bob Standard (methodology draft, v0.1)

The Hundred Bob Finder

An app that reads every licensed operator's prices, works out how likely each outcome really is, finds where it is cheapest to back, tells you in shillings what comes back to M-Pesa if it wins, and opens that operator's own app or site with the slip ready. It never touches your money.

§ 1

What the app does, and what it refuses to promise

The brief asks for the bets most likely to win and the real return after charges. Both are computable. The trap is pretending they point the same way.

Every operator publishes a price for every outcome, and every price hides two things: the operator's opinion of how likely the outcome is, and the operator's margin on top. Strip the margin and average across operators, and you have the best available estimate of the true probability of the outcome. Compare that to the best price any operator is offering, run the winnings through the deposit and withdrawal excise, and you have the net return. Those three numbers, side by side for every outcome on the board, are the product.

What the app cannot do is make a high-probability bet a high-return bet. A 90% favourite pays little because it is a 90% favourite. So the Finder never shows one ranking. It shows probability, net return and expected value on every row, lets the bettor sort by any of them, and marks the rare rows where the best price on the market is better than the odds the outcome deserves.

Probability comes from the market, not from us

No prediction model, no tipster. The consensus of de-vigged prices across at least three operators is the estimate, and the number of operators behind it is printed beside it.

Returns are shown as cash to M-Pesa

Not "odds 1.85". The figure is what lands back in the wallet after the 5% deposit excise, the operator's price, and the 5% withdrawal excise, for the amount the bettor actually typed.

Expected value is always visible

Every row carries the EV of KES 100 sent. It is negative almost everywhere. The app says so rather than hiding it under a green button.

Same evidence standard as the scorecard

Every odds observation carries source, timestamp, archive pointer and collector. The dataset the app runs on is the same open CSV the Hundred Bob Standard publishes.

The claim the app must never make

"Bets that have the highest probability of winning" reads, to a bettor, as "bets that will win". The app can rank by probability honestly only if it prints, on the same screen, that a 78% outcome loses one time in four and that backing it at the best price on the market still returns less than was sent, on average. The design below puts that sentence on the detail screen of every bet. Remove it and the product becomes a tipster with better maths.

§ 2

The pipeline

Seven stages between an operator's odds page and the row on the bettor's phone. Each is a separate service with its own table, so a failure in one is visible rather than silent.

§ 3

The probability engine

"How likely is this to win" has a defensible answer that needs no model of football: the market's own opinion, with the margin removed.

An operator quoting 1.50 / 4.20 / 6.50 on a 1X2 market is saying the outcomes are worth 66.7%, 23.8% and 15.4%. Those sum to 105.9%, and the extra 5.9% is the margin. Dividing each by the total gives probabilities that sum to one: 62.9%, 22.5%, 14.5%. That is the operator's de-vigged view. Do it for every operator quoting the fixture and take the median, and you have a consensus that no single operator's pricing error can drag around.

Three refinements the first version ships with

  1. Longshot correction. Operators load margin unevenly: favourites are priced near fair, longshots far from it. Dividing by the total spreads the margin evenly and so overstates longshot probability. The engine uses the power method (raise each implied probability to a fitted exponent k so they sum to one), which pushes margin onto the longshots where it actually sits. k is fitted per operator per month from settled results, and published.
  2. Minimum three operators. A consensus of one is just that operator's margin-stripped price. Rows with fewer than three quoting operators show the probability in grey with the count, and never enter the "most likely" sort.
  3. Spread as a confidence signal. If the de-vigged views range from 58% to 71%, the operators disagree and the number is soft. The app shows the range on the detail screen, and the ranker penalises wide spreads so a soft 70% does not outrank a firm 66%.

§ 4

The returns engine

The Hundred Bob Standard measures what an average shilling is worth. The Finder answers the bettor's question: if I send this amount and this bet wins, what comes back?

A bettor sends D shillings from M-Pesa. The Finance Act 2025 takes 5% on the way in, so the stake is 0.95 D. If the bet wins at decimal price O, the wallet holds 0.95 D × O. Withdrawing takes another 5%, on winnings and the returned stake alike, so the cash back to M-Pesa is 0.9025 D × O. Profit is that minus what was sent. The bet only pays if O exceeds 1.108, and that is before the operator's margin has said anything.

Why the "value" flag is rare and should stay rare

An outcome is flagged as value when the best price on the market is above its fair odds by enough to clear both excises, so the EV of a hundred bob is positive. On a board where every operator prices the same way, that never happens. It happens when one operator lags a team-news move, mis-keys a price, or runs a promotion that genuinely cuts margin. Those windows are short and operators close accounts that hit them repeatedly. The app should show the flag, show the EV, and say plainly that the price may be gone by the time the bet is placed.

§ 5

The bet finder, working

The engines above, running on an illustrative board of real clubs. Move the amount, the sort and the leagues; every figure, and the pick, recomputes from the raw prices in the table's own data.

The board names the bet it would take, at the top, and says why in one sentence. The rule is fixed and printed on the card: the highest expected value among outcomes priced by at least three operators, ties to the likelier outcome. On most boards the winner of that rule still has a negative EV, and the card then says "least costly, not profitable" rather than "best bet". When an operator's price beats the fair odds by more than the two excise charges, the card changes colour and says value was found.

The live board opens on the leagues every operator prices, because only those carry a full consensus; "All leagues" widens it and the choice is remembered on this phone only. The fraction beside each league is how many operators price it; a league priced by fewer than three cannot carry a consensus, and its rows show the probability in grey.

§ 7

  

    
Capturing the prices

    
The scraping is the least interesting and most fragile part. Design it to fail loudly and to leave evidence.

    

      

        
C1
One adapter per operator

        

          
Does
Loads the public pre-match pages in a headless browser, extracts fixture, market, outcome and price, and writes one observation row per price with the five evidence fields. Also saves the rendered HTML and a screenshot to the archive, keyed by the observation id.

          
Cadence
Every fixture at T−60 minutes for the scorecard's basket, plus every 15 minutes for the day's board so the app is never more than a quarter-hour stale. The capture time is printed on every screen.

          
Fails how
Loudly. An adapter that returns fewer than 80% of yesterday's fixture count, or any market whose prices do not sum to more than 1, is marked degraded and its rows are excluded from consensus until a human looks.

        

      

      

        
C2
Matching the same fixture across operators

        

          
Does
Normalises team names through an alias table ("Gor Mahia", "Gor Mahia FC", "GOR MAHIA"), then joins on competition, both teams and a kick-off within 30 minutes. A match is only accepted when both teams and the kick-off agree.

          
Fails how
Into quarantine. Anything that matches on one team only, or on the name but a different kick-off, goes to a review queue. A wrong match would blend two different games into one probability, which is the worst silent error the system can make.

        

      

      

        
C3
Conduct

        

          
Rules
Public pages only; never a logged-in session, never a bettor's credentials. One request every few seconds per operator, identified by a named user agent with a contact address. Honour robots exclusions and stop on request while the dispute is worked out. Capture prices, never copy editorial content.

          
Legal read
Odds are facts about a public offer and reproducing them for comparison is the same act every odds-comparison site performs. The exposure is in the operator terms of use, not in copyright; a Kenyan advocate should read the terms of every operator before its adapter goes live, and the finding is recorded beside the adapter.

§ 8

  

    
What the app is accountable for

    
A product that tells people how likely things are must publish how often it was right.

    
    
      
  • PUBLISHEDCalibration, monthly. Of the outcomes the app called 70–80% likely, how many won? A calibration table by decile, on a public page, with n. If the 70–80% bucket wins 63% of the time, the probability engine is wrong and the page says so.
    • 
      
  • PUBLISHEDRealised return. If a bettor had sent KES 100 on every row the app ranked first each day, what would have come back? Printed as a running figure on the home screen. It will be below 100. That is the point.
    • 
      
  • NEVERNo affiliate links, no revenue share, no tracked referral codes, no "sponsored" rows. The "Open Book A" button is a plain link that earns nothing. The moment the app earns from where a bettor is sent, its ranking is an advertisement.
    • 
      
  • NEVERNo money in, no money out, no winnings held, no operator account connected, no bettor password anywhere. The app knows what the operators are charging; it never knows what the bettor has.
    • 
      
  • NEVERNo push notifications for "hot" bets, no streaks, no confetti on a win. The app is consulted, it does not summon.
    • 
      
  • GATESelf-exclusion check on first run: a bettor who reports being on any operator's exclusion list is shown the calibration page and the realised-return figure, and nothing else.

§ 9

  

    
Hand-off to the operator

    
The app finds the bet. The operator takes it. Between the two, nothing crosses but a slip the bettor can read.

    
This is a deliberate boundary, not a missing feature. An app that accepts deposits is a payment business under the National Payment System Act; one that transmits bets may be an intermediary under the Gambling Control Act 2025; one that holds winnings is both. Each of those brings a licence, a regulator, a compliance function and a class of dispute the product does not need. By handing off, the Finder stays what it is: a measurement tool that tells a bettor where a price is good and what it is worth after the charges. The bet, the money and the account are the operator's, exactly as they are today.


    

    
§ 9.1 — What the slip carries

    
Operator, fixture, market, outcome, the price the Finder saw and the minute it saw it, the amount the bettor typed, and the charge chain for that amount. The app opens the operator in whichever of three ways the operator allows, best first:

    
    
      
  1. A link to the event with the selection marked, where the operator's app or site accepts one. The bettor lands on the right market with the outcome highlighted, types the stake, and confirms at the operator's live price.
    2. 
      
  2. A link to the event page, where only that is supported. The slip card stays on the bettor's screen as a notification so the selection can be read while the operator's page is open.
    3. 
      
  3. Copy the slip and open the site, for operators with no deep links at all. The card is plain text: "Book A · Fixture 3 · 1X2 · Home · seen 1.52 at 09:40".
    4. 
    

    
Which of the three an operator gets is recorded in its adapter and shown on the price sheet, because a two-tap hand-off and a copy-and-find hand-off are different products to a bettor on a bus.


    
§ 9.2 — What the app keeps about the bettor

    
Nothing that it does not need for the calibration table. The app does not know whether the bettor placed the bet, for how much, or what came back. Optionally, and off by default, a bettor can mark a slip "I placed this" after the hand-off, and the app then pairs it with the public result to show a personal realised-return figure. That record lives on the phone only, is never uploaded, and clears with the app. It exists because "what has this actually returned me" is the most useful number in the product, and the bettor is the only one who can supply half of it.


    
§ 9.3 — Guardrails that survive the boundary

    
    
      
  • ALWAYSThe sentence travels with the slip. "A 63% outcome loses about one time in three" is on the hand-off card, above the button, in the same words as the detail screen.
    • 
      
  • ALWAYSThe price is labelled as seen, with the time. The operator prices the bet live; the Finder's figure is up to fifteen minutes old and the card says so, so a moved price is not a surprise.
    • 
      
  • DEFAULT ONRealised return first. A bettor who has turned on the personal record sees their running figure at the top of the hand-off card before the button. Off by default because the record is.
    • 
      
  • HONESTNo caps, no loss pauses. The app cannot enforce a spending limit on money it never sees. Limits and self-exclusion are the operator's and the regulator's; the app links to each operator's limit-setting page from its price sheet and to the national self-exclusion route from settings, and does not pretend to more.
    • 
      
  • NEVERNo push notifications, no "price is moving" alerts, no re-open prompts. The app is consulted. A bettor who wants an alert on a value price asks for it in § 11's alerts phase and gets one that states the EV.
    • 
      
  • GATEAdvertising-rules read. An app that sends bettors to operators, even unpaid, sits near the Gambling Control Act's advertising provisions. A Kenyan advocate confirms before launch that a comparison tool with no operator money is outside them, and that reading is published beside the independence covenant.
    • 
    


    
§ 9.4 — The hand-off, in the prototype

    
"Take this to the operator" under the charge chain in § 5 builds the card for the selected row; the accumulator builder in § 10 does the same for a slip of several legs. The buttons are inert here, since there is no operator to open, but the card is exactly what the phone would carry across.

  






  
§ 10

  

    
The accumulator builder

    
Multibets are where Kenyan bettors put their money and where the margin does its damage. The builder exists so that a bettor who is going to build one anyway sees the compounding happen, leg by leg, before carrying the slip to the operator.

    
An accumulator differs from a handful of singles in three ways the design has to respect. Every leg must be placed at the same operator, so the best price is the best combined price across books, not the best price per leg. The probability that it pays is the product of the legs' probabilities, and the operator's margin compounds the same way. And the legs must be independent: two outcomes from one fixture are not a bet, they are a contradiction or a certainty, and the builder refuses them.


    
§ 10.1 — Rules the builder enforces

    
    
      
  • ALWAYSOne outcome per fixture. Adding a second leg from a fixture already in the slip is refused with the reason shown.
    • 
      
  • ALWAYSPriced per book, best book chosen. The builder multiplies each operator's prices for the chosen legs and picks the operator with the highest product. An operator missing any leg is excluded. The table of all four is shown, so the bettor sees how far apart they are.
    • 
      
  • ALWAYSCompounding shown after each leg. Probability of all legs winning, fair combined odds, the margin on the whole slip beside the average margin per leg, and the EV of KES 100. The margin figure grows with every leg; the builder lets it be watched.
    • 
      
  • ALWAYSThe sentence. "All five legs must win. That happens about once in 12 tries." Placed above the button, in the same words on the phone and in the prototype.
    • 
      
  • LIMITEight legs. Beyond that, at any realistic per-leg margin, the operator keeps most of the stake in expectation, and the app should not be the tool that builds it.
    • 
      
  • NEVERNo suggested accumulators, no "popular combos", no "boost" banners, no cash-out. The app builds what the bettor chooses and prices it honestly. The Standard's D1 measures whether "odds boosts" cut margin or merely move it; the builder does not carry them.
    • 
    


    
§ 10.2 — How the hand-off handles it

    
The § 9 hand-off carries every leg for the chosen operator, in that operator's own market names, with the combined price the Finder saw and the time. The operator's app prices all the legs live; if one has moved, the bettor sees the new combined price there before confirming, and the Finder's card is still on screen to compare against. The app shows the compounded margin before the hand-off precisely because the operator's slip will not.

§ 11

  

    
Build sequence

    
The scorecard's Phase 1 margin tracker is the Finder's Phase 0. Build the capture once and let both products run on it.

    

      
Phase 0
Capture and open data
Adapters for the operators whose public feeds permit it, the alias table, the archive, and the CSV publish. Built 2026-09-04: Betika, SportyBet and Odibets, football 1X2 within 48 hours, about 8,000 prices on 1,600 fixtures per capture, 146 of them priced by all three; SportPesa refused by its bot challenge and left out on purpose. The live page runs on it every 30 minutes.

      
Phase 1
Probability and returns, as a web table
The § 5 finder on live data, on a plain web page, with the basic de-vig. Start collecting settled results the same day so the power exponent can be fitted by the end of the phase.

      
Phase 2
The Android app, with hand-off
The three screens of § 6, English and Kiswahili, and the § 9 hand-off: event links with the selection marked where operators support them, event links or copied slips elsewhere. Calibration page goes live with the first month of results.

      
Phase 3
The accumulator builder
§ 10 as a display and a hand-off: per-book combined prices, compounding shown leg by leg, the slip carried across with every leg. Ships once the calibration table has held for one month, since a multibet's probability is only as good as its legs'.

      
Phase 4
Alerts, opt-in, EV stated
A bettor may ask to be told when a chosen outcome's best price rises above fair odds. The alert states the EV and the likely lifetime of the price. No other notification exists.

      
Standing
One dataset, two products
The Finder shows a bettor today's prices; the Standard shows the public each operator's price over a year. Same observations, same evidence fields, same refusal of operator money.
